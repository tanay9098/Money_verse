## LESSON 9: Smart shopping

- **Title:** Comparing before you buy
- **Slug:** comparing-before-you-buy
- **Summary:** Learn to compare price, size, and quality so your coins go further.
- **Body:** Two items can look alike and cost different amounts. Smart shoppers check what they get for the coins, not just the price tag. A bigger pack may cost less for each piece, but only if you will really use it all.
- **Difficulty:** easy
- **Topic:** Smart shopping
- **Age:** 8 to 12
- **Order:** 9
- **Pass percent:** 66
- **Objectives:**
  1. You can compare two items using price and amount.
  2. You can tell when a bigger pack is a good deal.
  3. You can explain why the cheapest tag is not always the best choice.
- **Sections:**
  1. id `look-at-both` · heading **Look at both sides** · picture Scales · text: Before buying, ask two questions: how much does it cost, and how much do I get? A 6-coin pack of 10 pencils costs less for each pencil than a 3-coin single pencil.
  2. id `only-if-you-use-it` · heading **A deal you will use** · picture Cart · text: A 12-coin pack of six juices is cheaper per juice. If you only need one today, the extra five might go to waste. A deal is only a deal when you use it.
  3. id `quality-counts` · heading **Quality counts too** · picture Light bulb · text: A very cheap notebook that falls apart in a week costs more in the end. Think about how long something will last, not just the price today.
- **Quiz:**
  - Q1 id `per-pencil` · "A pack of 10 pencils costs 6 coins. One pencil costs 3 coins. Which is cheaper for each pencil?"
    - The pack of 10 ✅ (Six coins for ten pencils is less than one coin each.)
    - The single pencil (One pencil at 3 coins costs more for each pencil.)
    - They cost the same (The pack gives more pencils for the coins.)
  - Q2 id `big-pack` · "When is a big pack a good deal?"
    - When you will use all of it ✅ (A deal helps only if nothing goes to waste.)
    - Always, because it is bigger (Extra you never use is wasted coins.)
    - Only when it is the most expensive (Price alone does not make something a good deal.)
  - Q3 id `cheap-notebook` · "A 2-coin notebook breaks in a week. A 4-coin notebook lasts all term. Which gives better value?"
    - The 4-coin notebook ✅ (It lasts much longer, so it costs less over time.)
    - The 2-coin notebook (You would buy it again and again.)
    - Neither (Both are items, but one lasts much longer.)
- **Reward:** 6 coins, 20 XP

## LESSON 10: Giving and sharing

- **Title:** Giving and sharing
- **Slug:** giving-and-sharing
- **Summary:** See how giving time or coins helps others, and how to plan for it.
- **Body:** Money is not only for buying things for yourself. People give to friends, families, and good causes. Planning a little to give means you can help without running short on the things you need.
- **Difficulty:** easy
- **Topic:** Giving
- **Age:** 8 to 12
- **Order:** 10
- **Pass percent:** 66
- **Objectives:**
  1. You can name ways to give besides spending coins.
  2. You can plan a small amount to give.
- **Sections:**
  1. id `ways-to-give` · heading **Ways to give** · picture Coin · text: You can give coins, but also time, a helping hand, or a handmade card. A thoughtful gift does not need to cost a lot.
  2. id `plan-to-give` · heading **Plan a little to give** · picture Savings jar · text: Some people set aside a small part of their coins for giving, such as 2 out of every 10. That way giving is planned, and needs are still covered first.
- **Quiz:**
  - Q1 id `ways` · "Which of these is a way to give that costs no coins?"
    - Helping a neighbor carry shopping ✅ (Time and help are gifts too.)
    - Buying the most expensive toy (That costs coins.)
    - Keeping everything for yourself (That is not giving.)
  - Q2 id `plan` · "Why plan a small amount to give?"
    - So you can help and still pay for your needs ✅ (A plan keeps both goals safe.)
    - So you never save anything (Planning to give does not stop saving.)
    - So you spend more on wants (Giving is different from spending on wants.)
- **Reward:** 6 coins, 20 XP

## LESSON 11: Savings that grow

- **Title:** Savings that grow
- **Slug:** savings-that-grow
- **Summary:** Meet simple interest: a small thank-you a bank pays for keeping coins safe.
- **Body:** When you keep coins in a savings account, the bank may add a little extra, called interest. The longer coins stay, the more extra they can earn. The extra is a reward for patience.
- **Difficulty:** medium
- **Topic:** Saving
- **Age:** 9 to 12
- **Order:** 11
- **Pass percent:** 66
- **Objectives:**
  1. You can say what interest is.
  2. You can work out simple interest on a small amount.
- **Sections:**
  1. id `what-is-interest` · heading **What is interest?** · picture Jar · text: Interest is extra money paid for saving. If 100 game coins earn 5 coins a year, the interest is 5 coins.
  2. id `time-helps` · heading **Time helps** · picture Calendar · text: At 5 coins a year, 100 coins earn 5 after one year and 10 after two. Leaving coins alone longer means more extra.
- **Quiz:**
  - Q1 id `define` · "What is interest on savings?"
    - Extra money added for keeping coins saved ✅ (That is the bank's thank-you for saving.)
    - A fee for saving (Interest is paid to you, not by you.)
    - Money you must give away (Interest is a reward, not a gift you owe.)
  - Q2 id `two-years` · "100 coins earn 5 coins a year. How much interest after two years?"
    - 10 coins ✅ (5 + 5 = 10.)
    - 5 coins (That is only one year.)
    - 20 coins (That is more than the rate gives.)
- **Reward:** 8 coins, 25 XP
- **Note:** Use Difficulty **medium** and Age **9–12** here.
